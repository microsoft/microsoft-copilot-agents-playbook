🍝 Pasta al Pomodoro (Spaghetti al Pomodoro)

One of the most authentic and beloved dishes in Italian cuisine, spaghetti al pomodoro is the perfect example of how a few high-quality ingredients can create something buonissimo! 🇮🇹

Ingredients (4 servings)

  • 360 g spaghetti
  • 800 g canned peeled tomatoes (pomodori pelati)
  • 1 clove garlic
  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • Fresh basil
  • Salt
  • Freshly ground black pepper 1

Method

  1. In a saucepan, gently heat a generous drizzle of extra virgin olive oil with the garlic clove cut in half and with the germ removed. 1

  2. Add the whole peeled tomatoes, season with salt, cover, and cook over low heat for about 30 minutes. 1

  3. Remove the garlic and pass the tomatoes through a food mill (passaverdure) to obtain a smooth but slightly pulpy sauce. 1

  4. Return the sauce to the saucepan and cook uncovered for another 10 minutes so it thickens slightly. 1

  5. Meanwhile, cook the spaghetti in salted boiling water until al dente (firm to the bite). Drain and transfer directly to the sauce. 1

  6. Toss briefly to coat the pasta evenly. Serve immediately with fresh basil and a little freshly ground black pepper. 1

Chef's Tip 👨‍🍳

For a truly authentic result, use high-quality Italian San Marzano-style peeled tomatoes and a fruity extra virgin olive oil. The simplicity of the dish means the quality of the ingredients makes all the difference. Pochi ingredienti, grande sapore! ("Few ingredients, great flavor.")

Prompt 3: What tools and ingredients do I need to make a great risotto?

Evaluators: RelevanceCoherence

Response:

Ah, risotto! 🍚 One of the great treasures of Italian cuisine. The secret is not fancy equipment, but using the right ingredients and respecting the technique. Piano piano (slowly, slowly), as an Italian nonna would say. 😊

Essential Tools 🔧

1. Wide, heavy-bottomed pan or sauté pan

  • The most important tool.
  • A wide pan helps the rice cook evenly and lets excess moisture evaporate properly.
  • Stainless steel, aluminum, or enameled cast iron all work well.

2. Medium pot for the broth

  • Keep your broth hot throughout cooking.
  • Adding cold broth slows cooking and can affect the texture.

3. Wooden spoon or silicone spatula

  • Gentle stirring helps release starch without damaging the grains.

4. Ladle

  • For adding broth one scoop at a time.

5. Fine grater (optional but highly recommended)

  • Essential for freshly grating Parmigiano Reggiano during the final mantecatura (the finishing step where butter and cheese create a creamy texture).

Essential Ingredients 🛒

1. Risotto Rice

Choose one of these Italian varieties:

🥇 Carnaroli - Often considered the king of risotto rice. - Produces a creamy risotto while keeping a firm center.

🥈 Vialone Nano - Excellent absorption of flavors. - Common in Veneto.

🥉 Arborio - Widely available. - Good choice for beginners.

2. Quality Broth

  • Chicken broth for classic risottos.
  • Vegetable broth for mushroom or vegetable risottos.
  • Homemade is ideal, but a well-made store-bought broth is fine.

3. Onion or Shallot

  • Finely chopped for the soffritto (the flavor base).

4. Dry White Wine

  • Adds acidity and complexity.
  • Examples: Pinot Grigio, Soave, Vermentino.

5. Butter

  • Used both for cooking and finishing.

6. Parmigiano Reggiano

  • Freshly grated.
  • Adds richness and umami.

7. Extra Virgin Olive Oil

  • Often used for the initial sautéing.

The Golden Formula for Great Risotto ⭐

For about 4 servings:

  • 320 g Carnaroli rice
  • 1 liter hot broth
  • 1 small onion or shallot
  • ½ cup dry white wine
  • 30 g butter for cooking
  • 30-50 g cold butter for finishing
  • 50-80 g grated Parmigiano Reggiano
  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper

Professional Chef Tips 👨‍🍳

Toast the rice (tostatura)

Before adding liquid, cook the rice in the pan for 1-2 minutes until the grains become slightly translucent around the edges. This helps the rice keep its shape.

Keep the broth hot

Always add hot broth, one ladle at a time.

Stir with purpose

You don't need to stir constantly. Stir regularly enough to release starch and prevent sticking.

Finish off the heat

The best risottos are finished with cold butter and Parmigiano after removing the pan from the heat. This creates the famous creamy texture Italians call all'onda ("like a wave"), where the risotto gently flows when the pan is shaken.

Use the best cheese you can afford

A good Parmigiano Reggiano often improves a risotto more than expensive wine or fancy add-ins.

My ideal starter kit: Carnaroli rice, homemade vegetable broth, shallot, dry white wine, Parmigiano Reggiano 24-month aged, and a wide stainless-steel sauté pan. With those few ingredients, you can make a buonissimo basic risotto and then build endless variations like mushroom, saffron (Risotto alla Milanese), seafood, or asparagus. 🍄🧀🌿

Risotto alla Milanese (4 persone)

Ingredienti

  • 320 g di riso Carnaroli
  • 60 g di formaggio grattugiato
  • 60 g di burro
  • 20 g di midollo di vitello
  • 1,5 l di brodo di carne
  • 1 cipolla piccola
  • 1 bustina di zafferano (oppure pistilli) 1

Procedimento

  1. Preparate il brodo di carne. Se usate pistilli di zafferano, lasciateli in infusione in una tazza di brodo tiepido per almeno un'ora, così rilasceranno colore e aroma. 1
  2. Estraete il midollo dai dischi d'osso e tagliatelo a pezzetti. 1
  3. In una casseruola ampia fate sciogliere 30 g di burro con il midollo. Aggiungete la cipolla tritata finemente e fatela appassire a fuoco molto basso fino a renderla trasparente. 1
  4. Unite il riso e tostatelo mescolando finché diventa lucido. 1
  5. Cuocete il risotto aggiungendo un mestolo di brodo caldo alla volta, aspettando che venga assorbito prima di aggiungerne altro. A metà cottura incorporate lo zafferano sciolto nel brodo. 1
  6. Dopo circa 15-18 minuti, quando il riso è cotto al dente, togliete dal fuoco e mantecate con il restante burro e il formaggio grattugiato. 1
  7. Coprite per un paio di minuti, quindi servite immediatamente ben caldo. 1

Consiglio dello chef 👨‍🍳

Usa una casseruola larga e pesante: il riso cuocerà in modo più uniforme e svilupperà meglio quella consistenza all’onda (cremosa e leggermente fluida) tipica dei grandi risotti milanesi.
